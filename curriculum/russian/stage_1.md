# Russian Stage 1 — 100 Essential Everyday Words & Expressions

## Goal

Stage 1 is designed to get a complete beginner communicating quickly **without requiring Cyrillic first**.

For every item, learn in this order:

1. **Meaning**
2. **Pronunciation**
3. **Say it aloud**
4. **Recognize the Cyrillic**
5. **Use it in a tiny sentence**

The Latin-letter column below is now a **pronunciation guide**, not a strict transliteration. This matters because Russian spelling and everyday pronunciation do not always match exactly.

## Pronunciation Key

- **CAPITAL letters** = the stressed syllable: `si-VOD-nya`.
- **kh** = the Russian **х** sound, similar to the sound in Scottish *loch*.
- **zh** = the sound in *mea**s**ure*.
- **shch** = the Russian **щ** sound.
- **y** can represent Russian sounds that do not map perfectly to English.
- An apostrophe after a consonant, such as `t'` or `n'`, means the consonant is **softened**.

### The “b-looking” letter: ь

The character **ь** is the **soft sign**. It is **not pronounced as its own sound**.

Instead, it usually softens the consonant immediately before it. For example:

- **знать** — `znat'` — to know
- **говорить** — `ga-va-REET'` — to speak
- **день** — `dyen'` — day
- **ночь** — `noch'` — night

In many Russian infinitive verbs, you will see **-ть** at the end. Do **not** say a separate sound for **ь**. Say a softened **t** at the end instead.

- **Day 1:** Items 1–20

# Important Pronunciation Changes to Notice

These are examples where a strict transliteration can mislead a beginner:

- **сегодня** is spelled with **г**, but is pronounced approximately **si-VOD-nya**.
- **что** is normally pronounced **shto**, not “chto.”
- **конечно** is commonly pronounced **ka-NYESH-na**.
- **завтра** has a devoiced consonant in ordinary speech, giving roughly **ZAF-tra**.
- **друг** ends with a written **г**, but the final consonant is devoiced, so it sounds roughly like **drook**.
- Unstressed Russian **о** often sounds closer to **a**, which is why words such as **хорошо** and **когда** do not sound exactly as their spelling suggests.

# 10 Sentence Patterns to Master

### 1. Я хочу + noun / action
**ya kha-CHOO...** — I want...

- Я хочу кофе. — **ya kha-CHOO KO-fe.** — I want coffee.

### 2. Мне нужно + noun / action
**mnye NOOZH-na...** — I need...

- Мне нужна помощь. — **mnye noozh-NA PO-mashch'.** — I need help.

### 3. Где + place?
**gdye...?** — Where is...?

- Где туалет? — **gdye too-a-LYET?** — Where is the restroom?

### 4. Что это?
**shto EH-ta?** — What is this?

### 5. Я не понимаю
**ya ni pa-ni-MA-yu.** — I don't understand.

### 6. Вы говорите + language?
**vy ga-va-REE-tye...?** — Do you speak...?

### 7. Я из + place
**ya iz...** — I'm from...

### 8. Меня зовут + name
**mi-NYA za-VOOT...** — My name is...

### 9. Я люблю + noun / action
**ya lyu-BLYU...** — I love / like...

### 10. Можно + noun / action?
**MOZH-na...?** — May I? / Is it possible?

# Stage 1 Speaking Drills

## Drill A — Instant Recall

Look only at the English and say the Russian aloud.

## Drill B — Russian Recognition

Look at the Cyrillic and say:

1. what it means;
2. the pronunciation;
3. the word aloud.

## Drill C — Five-Second Rule

For each English prompt, give yourself five seconds to answer in Russian. If you cannot answer within five seconds, mark it for review.

## Drill D — Build Tiny Sentences

Use the 100 items to create short combinations:

- Я хочу чай. — I want tea.
- Мне нужна помощь. — I need help.
- Где ресторан? — Where is the restaurant?
- Я не понимаю. — I don't understand.
- Это хорошо. — This is good.
- Я работаю сегодня. — I work today.

# Stage 1 Mini Conversations

## Conversation 1 — Meeting Someone

**A:** Привет!  
*pree-VYET!*  
Hi!

**B:** Привет!  
*pree-VYET!*  
Hi!

**A:** Меня зовут Алекс.  
*mi-NYA za-VOOT Aleks.*  
My name is Alex.

**B:** Меня зовут Анна.  
*mi-NYA za-VOOT Anna.*  
My name is Anna.

**A:** Как дела?  
*kak di-LA?*  
How are you?

**B:** Хорошо, спасибо.  
*kha-ra-SHO, spa-SEE-ba.*  
Good, thank you.

## Conversation 2 — Asking for Help

**A:** Извините.  
*iz-vi-NEE-tye.*  
Excuse me.

**B:** Да?  
*da?*  
Yes?

**A:** Где туалет?  
*gdye too-a-LYET?*  
Where is the restroom?

**B:** Там, направо.  
*tam, na-PRA-va.*  
There, to the right.

**A:** Спасибо!  
*spa-SEE-ba!*  
Thank you!

## Conversation 3 — When You Don't Understand

**A:** Вы говорите по-английски?  
*vy ga-va-REE-tye pa ang-LEEY-ski?*  
Do you speak English?

**B:** Немного.  
*ni-MNO-ga.*  
A little.

**A:** Я не понимаю. Повторите, пожалуйста.  
*ya ni pa-ni-MA-yu. paf-ta-REE-tye, pa-ZHA-lus-ta.*  
I don't understand. Repeat, please.

# Stage 1 Self-Test

You are ready to move on when you can:

- [ ] Recognize at least **80 of the 100** items from Russian → English.
- [ ] Produce at least **70 of the 100** from English → Russian.
- [ ] Say the 10 sentence patterns without looking.
- [ ] Introduce yourself.
- [ ] Ask where something is.
- [ ] Ask someone to repeat themselves.
- [ ] Say that you understand or do not understand.
- [ ] Ask for something you want.
- [ ] Ask for something you need.
- [ ] Complete the three mini conversations with minimal help.

Once you can recall roughly **80% comfortably**, begin Stage 2 while continuing to review weak Stage 1 words.
